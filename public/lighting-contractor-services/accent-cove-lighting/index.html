Cove Lighting

Cove lighting bounces light off the ceiling for a soft, even glow with no visible fixtures. It's usually done with LED strip lighting in a channel, hidden behind the lip of a tray ceiling or crown molding. The key details are placing the strip so you don't see dots of light, choosing a warm color temperature, and running long strips with the power feeds they need to stay evenly bright.

Accent Lighting

Accent lighting draws attention to things: shelves, art, stone walls or architectural details. We use small LED fixtures, strips and picture lights, and adjustable track lighting for art that changes. Toe-kick and stair lighting doubles as safe night lighting. Wall sconces can also serve as accent lights beside a fireplace or artwork.

Drivers, Switching and Dimming

Most accent and cove lighting runs on low voltage and needs a driver, which we locate where it's hidden but can still be reached for service. We put these lights on their own dimmer so they can run low in the evening, and they can be tied into smart controls.

What Affects the Cost

Length of the runs, how many zones, whether the woodwork is in place and the dimming method shape the price. Coordinating with your carpenter during a build is easier and less expensive.

Color and Brightness

For most homes, warm white LED strips around 2700K to 3000K look best in coves and on shelves. Tunable-white strips let you shift from warmer to cooler light, and color-changing strips can be fun in a media room or bar. Brightness should be modest; accent lighting works best as a soft layer, not the main light in the room. We'll help you choose the right strip and density for each spot.

What's the difference between cove and accent lighting?

Cove lighting is indirect light bounced off a ceiling. Accent lighting highlights specific things like art, shelves and architecture.

Can cove lighting be added to an existing tray ceiling?

Usually. We run power to the tray and install LED strip in a channel behind the lip.

Is accent lighting dimmable?

Yes, with a dimmable driver and a compatible dimmer.

Where does the driver go?

Somewhere hidden but reachable, such as inside a cabinet, a closet or the attic above a tray ceiling, so it can be replaced without tearing anything apart.
