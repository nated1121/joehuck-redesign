When You Need an Electrician for an Appliance

  • You're switching from gas to an electric or induction range
  • The new range or dryer has a different plug than the old outlet
  • You're adding a wall oven, cooktop or second oven
  • You're installing an electric or heat pump water heater
  • The dishwasher, disposal or built-in microwave needs a new connection

Sizing the Circuit to the Appliance

Every appliance lists the circuit it needs on its nameplate and in the installation instructions. An induction range often needs 40 or 50 amps; a wall oven and cooktop combination may need separate circuits. We size the wire and breaker to the appliance, not a guess, and install the correct receptacle or hardwired connection. If your panel lacks capacity or space for the new circuit, we'll talk through the options, including a panel upgrade.

Three-Wire to Four-Wire Connections

Older homes often have three-prong range and dryer outlets. Current code requires four-wire circuits for new installations, with a separate ground and neutral. When a new appliance comes with a four-wire cord, we update the circuit and receptacle, or connect it correctly to an existing three-wire circuit where that's permitted.

Built-Ins and Kitchen Appliances

Dishwashers, disposals and built-in microwaves each typically need their own circuit and a proper connection. We coordinate with your appliance installer or cabinet crew. For a full kitchen, see kitchen electrical upgrades. For equipment like freezers and gym machines, see dedicated circuit installation.

What Affects the Cost

A like-for-like hookup on an existing correct circuit is quick. A new 240-volt circuit costs more, especially when the appliance is far from the panel.

Heat Pump Water Heaters and Other Upgrades

Heat pump water heaters, electric heat pumps and induction cooktops are replacing gas appliances in many homes. Each has specific circuit requirements, and several at once can add up to more than an older panel can carry. When you're planning more than one electric appliance, we'll run a load calculation for the whole group, so you know in advance whether your panel can handle all of them.

Questions

Good to Know.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Can I switch from a gas range to induction?

Yes, with a 240-volt circuit sized to the range, usually 40 or 50 amps. We'll check your panel first.

My new dryer plug doesn't fit the outlet. What now?

The dryer likely has a four-prong cord and your outlet is three-prong. We'll update the circuit or connect it the correct way.

Does a dishwasher need its own circuit?

It's typically required in new work and strongly recommended. We'll confirm what applies to your kitchen.
