Attic Fan Installation in Yardley, PA

On a July afternoon, an attic can reach well over 130 degrees. A powered attic fan pushes that heat out, which can ease the load on your air conditioning and help control moisture. We install and wire attic fans in Yardley-area homes, and repair the ones that have quit.

Signs an Attic Fan Could Help

  • Upstairs rooms stay hot in summer even with the AC running
  • The attic is stuffy, with no ridge or soffit venting
  • You see condensation or frost on roof sheathing in winter
  • Your existing attic fan hums but doesn't spin, or never turns on

Gable and Roof-Mounted Fans

Gable fans mount behind an existing gable vent and don't need a new opening in the roof, which makes them a simple install. Roof-mounted fans need a new opening and flashing; when one is needed, we work with your roofer, or wire a unit they've installed. Solar-powered fans don't need wiring at all, though they only run when the sun is out.

Before recommending a fan, we look at your attic's intake venting. A fan without enough fresh air coming in through the soffits can pull conditioned air from the house instead of outdoor air, which defeats the purpose.

Wiring and Controls

We wire the fan to a nearby circuit or a dedicated circuit, depending on the fan's draw, and install an adjustable thermostat so it runs only when the attic is hot. A humidistat can also run it when moisture builds up. Wiring runs neatly along the framing with proper support, and the fan has a local disconnect for service. We can add a switch or a smart control so you can turn it off from inside the house; our smart home wiring page covers those options.

Repairs

A fan that hums but won't spin usually has a failed motor or seized bearing. A fan that never runs may have a failed thermostat or a wiring problem. We diagnose it and repair or replace the motor or the whole unit. For cooling the rooms themselves, see ceiling fan installation.

What Affects the Cost

Gable fans cost less to install than roof-mounted units. The distance to a power source and the controls you choose also affect the price.

Safety Features

Attic fans should have a firestat that shuts the fan off if the attic gets extremely hot, which some local codes require. We install or check it as part of the job.

Good to Know.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Does an attic fan replace air conditioning?

No. It removes heat from the attic, which can reduce the load on your AC and keep upstairs rooms cooler.

What's the difference between an attic fan and a whole-house fan?

An attic fan ventilates only the attic. A whole-house fan pulls air through the living space and out through the attic.

Should my attic fan have a thermostat?

Yes, so it runs only when the attic needs it.
