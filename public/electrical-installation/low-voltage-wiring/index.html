When Low-Voltage Wiring Helps

  • Wi-Fi is weak in the office, basement or far rooms
  • You want wired connections for TVs, gaming or security cameras
  • A wall-mounted TV needs its cables hidden
  • A new thermostat needs more conductors than you have
  • You're remodeling and want to plan ahead

What We Run

We run Cat6 network cable to offices, TVs and Wi-Fi access points, terminated at wall plates and at a central location near your router. We also run thermostat wire, doorbell wire, speaker wire for built-in audio, and cable for cameras. For wall-mounted TVs, we install a recessed outlet and a code-approved pass-through so cords stay inside the wall. See switch and outlet relocation for the power side of a TV wall.

Doing It Right

Low-voltage cable has its own rules. It has to be kept apart from power wiring, or separated by an approved barrier, and it can't share a box with line-voltage connections. It should be run through proper openings with bushings, not stapled tight, and labeled at both ends. Poorly run cable is one of the things we most often clean up in finished basements.

Part of a Smart Home

Low-voltage wiring is the backbone of a reliable smart home. Video doorbells, cameras and access points all depend on it, and running it before the walls close is far easier than fishing it later.

What Affects the Cost

The number of runs, the distance to the central location and whether walls are open drive the price. Finished two-story homes take longer than a basement with open joists.

Planning a Home Network

A good home network starts in one place, usually near where the internet enters the house. From there, we run cable to the rooms and devices that need a wired connection: offices, TVs, gaming consoles, cameras and ceiling-mounted Wi-Fi access points. We label every cable at both ends and terminate them in wall plates and at a central patch location, so adding or troubleshooting a connection later is easy. If you're remodeling, running a few extra cables while the walls are open is inexpensive insurance.

Is wired internet really better than Wi-Fi?

For desks, TVs and access points, yes. It's faster and more consistent, and it frees up Wi-Fi for phones and laptops.

Can you hide TV cables in the wall?

Yes, with a recessed outlet and a code-approved low-voltage pass-through.

Can low-voltage cable run next to electrical wire?

It needs separation or an approved barrier, and it can't share a box with power connections.
