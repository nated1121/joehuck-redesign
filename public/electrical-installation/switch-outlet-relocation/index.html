Common Reasons to Relocate

  • You're mounting a TV and want the outlet hidden behind it
  • New cabinets, a vanity or built-ins cover an existing outlet or switch
  • A switch is on the hinge side of the door, behind it when it's open
  • Outlets are at the wrong height for a desk, counter or bed
  • A remodel moved a doorway or a wall

How We Move an Outlet or Switch

Most relocations use the existing circuit. We open the old box, extend the circuit to the new location through the wall, and install a new box and device there. The old box can't just be buried in the wall; code requires every splice to stay accessible, so we either leave it as a neat blank-covered junction box or reroute the wiring so the old box can be removed and patched.

For TV walls, we can install a recessed outlet and a low-voltage pass-through behind the TV so no cords show. For switches, moving to the latch side of a door or adding a second switch location often goes together. Controlling a light from two places is covered on our three-way switch wiring page.

Relocation During Remodels

If the walls are open, relocating devices is simple and inexpensive, so it's worth thinking about layout before the drywall goes up. For bigger changes, see electrical wiring installation. When a new location needs a brand-new circuit, see switch and outlet installation.

What Affects the Cost

Distance, wall construction (plaster takes longer than drywall), whether there's a fire block or header in the way, and whether the old box can be removed all affect the price. We'll tell you how much patching to expect.

Plan Before You Mount the TV

If you're wall-mounting a TV, decide on its height and position before we move the outlet. The outlet should sit behind the TV, within reach of the power cord but hidden by the screen. If you'll use a soundbar or a streaming box, we'll plan a second outlet or a larger recessed box so every device plugs in behind the screen and nothing hangs below it.

Questions

Good to Know.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Can you hide the TV cords in the wall?

Yes. We install a recessed outlet behind the TV and a code-approved pass-through for low-voltage cables.

What happens to the old outlet box?

It either stays as a covered junction box or is removed once the wiring is rerouted. Splices can never be hidden inside a wall.

Will you patch the drywall?

We keep openings small and neat. We can leave them ready for your painter or arrange patching.
