Homes in Bristol and What They Need

Bristol Borough's waterfront and Radcliffe Street are lined with 18th- and 19th-century homes, and the streets around Mill Street are full of older twins and rowhomes built for the town's mill and shipyard workers. Houses this old often have wiring from several eras behind the walls: some knob-and-tube, some cloth-covered cable, a fuse box or an overloaded panel, and circuits added by past owners. Bristol Township, which includes part of Levittown and Croydon, is mostly post-war homes with small original services and too few circuits.

Properties close to the river have another concern: moisture. Basements and low-lying areas need wiring and protection suited to damp locations, and equipment may need to sit higher than it does now.

Electrical Work We Do in Bristol

Our most common Bristol jobs are home rewiring for older borough homes, fuse box replacement, panel upgrades for post-war homes in the township, and electrical repair and troubleshooting. We also add GFCI protection in basements and kitchens, and handle code corrections for sales and rental inspections.

We work for small businesses along Mill Street and the Bristol Pike corridor too, with lighting, new circuits and repairs scheduled around business hours.

Around Bristol

We work throughout the borough and township, from the streets near the Bristol Riverside Theatre and the Grundy clock tower to the waterfront park and the neighborhoods along Route 13. Being based up the river in Yardley means we can schedule quickly and come back easily when a job runs in phases.

Common Electrical Issues in Bristol Homes

The issues we see most are older wiring in historic homes, ungrounded outlets, fuse boxes, overloaded circuits and amateur repairs from years past. In rowhomes and twins, tight spaces and shared walls make careful planning important. We'll explain what we find in plain language and give you options, safety items first.

Waterfront and Flood-Prone Properties

Homes near the river and Otter Creek can take on water in major storms. If your basement has flooded, anything electrical that was underwater should be inspected before it's used again, and panels or equipment may need to be raised.
