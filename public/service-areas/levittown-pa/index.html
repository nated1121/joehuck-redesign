What We Find in Levittown Homes

Most Levitt homes were built with small electrical services and just a few circuits. Over 70 years, owners have added air conditioning, bigger kitchens, laundry, finished additions and home offices, often by stretching the original circuits. The result is panels with no room, overloaded circuits, two-prong outlets and wiring that's been extended by several different hands.

These homes were built on concrete slabs with radiant heat in the floor, so there's no basement to run new wiring through. We route new circuits through attics, soffits and exterior walls instead, and we know where the good paths are in each model, from the Levittowner and Jubilee to the Country Clubber.

Common Electrical Issues in Levitt Homes

Besides small services, the issues we see most are two-prong outlets, circuits extended to additions by previous owners, and kitchens with too few circuits for modern appliances. We'll explain what we find and give you a plan you can do all at once or in stages.

How do you run new wiring in a Levitt home without a basement?

Through the attic, soffits and wall cavities. We know the common routes in the original Levitt models, which keeps wall damage to a minimum.

Does my Levittown home need a service upgrade?

If it still has its original service or a small panel with no open slots, very likely. We'll check your panel and load before recommending anything.
