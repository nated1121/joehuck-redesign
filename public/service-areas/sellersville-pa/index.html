Homes in Sellersville

Much of Sellersville's housing dates from the late 1800s through the mid-1900s: twins, row homes and single homes near Main Street and the old mill and factory areas, with newer neighborhoods on the edges of the borough. Older homes here often have wiring added over many decades, including knob-and-tube, cloth-covered cable and fuse boxes, and many have too few circuits and ungrounded outlets.

Electrical Work We Do in Sellersville

Our most common Sellersville jobs are home rewiring, panel upgrades, ground wire installation for ungrounded outlets and lighting repair. We also add GFCI protection in kitchens, baths and basements, install ceiling fans and fixtures, and handle code corrections for homes being sold in the Pennridge school district.

Rewiring Older Homes

Rewiring an older twin or single doesn't have to mean opening every wall. We route new cable through the basement and attic and existing wall cavities, working room by room so you have power in the rest of the house. When the service is too small for the new circuits, we upgrade the panel at the same time.

Around Sellersville

We work throughout the borough, from Main Street and the Sellersville Theater to the neighborhoods near Lake Lenape and the creek, and in nearby parts of West Rockhill. Sellersville is one of the farther towns we serve from our base in Yardley, so we schedule upper Bucks appointments together to keep them reliable.

Common Electrical Issues in Sellersville Homes

The issues we see most are old wiring, fuse boxes, ungrounded outlets and overloaded circuits in kitchens and bathrooms. We'll explain what we find in plain language and give you options, safety items first.

Adding Circuits and Outlets

Many older Sellersville homes were built with only one or two outlets per room. We add outlets and circuits where you need them, usually by fishing wire from the basement or attic, so you can stop relying on extension cords and power strips. Kitchens and bathrooms get GFCI protection, and new bedroom and living-room circuits get the arc-fault protection current code requires.
