Homes in Doylestown

The borough's historic district is full of Victorian, Federal and early 20th-century homes along streets like Court Street, State Street and Pine Street. Like other homes of that age, many were wired in stages over the past century, so it's common to find knob-and-tube, cloth-insulated cable, fuse boxes and ungrounded circuits behind original plaster. Careful home rewiring lets these homes keep their character while getting safe, modern wiring. The township's neighborhoods, mostly built from the 1970s onward, need more capacity, better lighting and circuits for new appliances and EVs.

Electrical Work We Do in Doylestown

Our most common Doylestown jobs are rewiring for historic homes, fuse box replacement, panel upgrades and chandelier and fixture installation in older homes with tall ceilings. We also install recessed lighting, add surge protection and handle code corrections for sales.

Working in Historic Homes

Historic homes need a careful approach. We plan cable routes through attics, basements and existing cavities to avoid damaging plaster and original trim, protect floors and woodwork while we work, and keep openings small and neat. When a project affects the exterior of a building in the historic district, check with the borough about any additional approvals before work starts.

Around Doylestown

We work throughout the borough, near landmarks like the Mercer Museum, Fonthill Castle, the Michener Art Museum and the County Theater, and in the township's neighborhoods toward Central Bucks. Doylestown is a longer drive from our base in Yardley, so we group appointments to keep scheduling reliable.

Common Electrical Issues in Doylestown Homes

In the borough, it's older wiring, fuse boxes and panels that have been added to over the years. In the township, it's capacity for modern loads, builder-grade lighting and basements ready to be finished. We'll explain what we find in plain language.
