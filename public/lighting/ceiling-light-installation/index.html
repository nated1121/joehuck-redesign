When to Call for a Ceiling Light

  • You're replacing old flush mounts or "boob lights" throughout the house
  • A room has only a switched outlet and no ceiling light
  • The new fixture is heavier than the old one
  • The existing box is loose, damaged or has no ground
  • The ceiling or fixture shows signs of heat or scorching

Replacing a Fixture

We remove the old fixture, check the box and wiring, and correct anything that isn't right: a loose or undersized box, brittle wire insulation from years of heat, or a missing ground. Then we mount the new fixture securely, level it and test it. If you're replacing several fixtures at once, it's efficient to do them in one visit.

Adding a Light Where There Isn't One

Many older homes have bedrooms and living rooms with a switched outlet instead of a ceiling light. We run a new switch leg from the wall switch to the ceiling through the attic or from below, install a properly supported box and mount the fixture. If you'd rather have a fan there, the box needs to be fan-rated; see ceiling fan installation. For multiple lights in one room, consider recessed lighting.

Heavier Fixtures

Standard boxes are rated for fixtures up to a certain weight. Heavier fixtures need a box and support rated for them. We check the weight before installing; for large, heavy fixtures, see chandelier installation.

What Affects the Cost

Straight swaps are quick, especially several in one visit. Adding a new light and switch leg, high ceilings and heavy fixtures take longer.

Updating Every Fixture at Once

Many homeowners replace all the builder-grade fixtures in a house in one visit: bedroom and hallway flush mounts, the dining light and the entry fixture. It's efficient, it gives the house a consistent look, and it's a good chance for us to check each box and connection. We'll haul away the old fixtures and packaging. If some of your fixtures are on dimmers, we'll make sure the new bulbs and dimmers are compatible.
