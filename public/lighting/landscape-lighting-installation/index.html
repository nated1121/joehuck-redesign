What Landscape Lighting Can Light

  • Walkways, driveways and steps
  • Trees, shrubs and garden beds (uplighting)
  • The front of the house and architectural features
  • Patios, decks and outdoor kitchens
  • Pool areas and water features

Low-Voltage Systems

Most landscape lighting runs on 12 volts from a transformer plugged into or wired to a GFCI-protected outdoor outlet. Low voltage is safer around the yard and lets fixtures be moved as plants grow. We size the transformer for the total load with room to add fixtures, plan cable runs to keep brightness even, and bury cable neatly so it doesn't get cut by edgers or aerators. If you need a new outlet for the transformer, see outdoor outlet installation.

Designing the Layout

Good landscape lighting lights what matters and leaves some darkness for contrast. We walk the property with you, usually at dusk, and plan path lights, uplights on trees and walls, and step and deck lights. Warm LED color temperature looks best on stone, brick and plants. We aim fixtures to avoid glare into windows and neighboring yards.

Timers and Controls

Transformers with photocells and timers, or astronomical clocks that follow sunset through the seasons, keep lights running without you touching them. Smart transformers can be controlled from your phone. For motion-activated or brighter lighting, see security and flood lighting. Around pools, low-voltage lighting follows specific rules; see pool electrical wiring.

What Affects the Cost

The number of fixtures, the fixture quality, the size of the property and how the cable is routed drive the price. Many homeowners start with the front and add more later.

Fixture Quality Matters

Landscape fixtures live outdoors year-round, so materials matter. Solid brass and copper fixtures last far longer than painted aluminum or plastic and develop a natural patina. Sealed LED fixtures resist moisture and insects. Quality connectors keep water out of connections, which is the most common cause of failed landscape lighting. We use fixtures and connectors built to last, and we'll show you what to expect from each option.

Maintaining Your System

Plants grow, mulch builds up and fixtures get bumped. An occasional check keeps lights aimed and working, and we can adjust your system as your landscaping changes.

Is low-voltage landscape lighting safe?

Yes. It runs on 12 volts from a transformer on a GFCI-protected outlet, which makes it much safer than line-voltage lighting in the yard.

Can I add more lights later?

Yes. We size the transformer with room to expand.

How are the lights controlled?

With a timer, photocell, astronomical clock or smart control, so they come on at dusk automatically.
