Signs of a Doorbell Wiring Problem

  • The video doorbell reboots, drops offline or shows low power
  • The chime hums, buzzes or doesn't ring
  • The button is dead, or the doorbell never worked in your house
  • You want a doorbell at a door that has no wiring
  • The old transformer is warm or hums

Choosing a Video Doorbell

Battery doorbells are simpler to install but need charging, and they can lag in cold weather. Wired doorbells stay powered and record continuously, which is why we recommend wiring whenever possible. Check that the doorbell fits your door trim, whether it needs a wedge or corner mount to see visitors, and whether its app and subscription suit you. We'll install the one you choose and make sure it gets the steady power it needs. For side and back doors, we can run new doorbell wire so every entry is covered.

Why Video Doorbells Need a Better Transformer

Traditional doorbells run on a small low-voltage transformer, often 10 or 16 volts, that only needs to power a chime briefly. Video doorbells need steady power, typically 16 to 24 volts, to run the camera, Wi-Fi and night vision. An old transformer can't keep up, so the doorbell drains its battery or restarts. We replace the transformer with the right one, located where it's accessible, and add the chime adapter or power kit your doorbell requires.

Installation and Repairs

We mount the doorbell at the right height and angle, connect it to properly sized wiring, and set up the chime. If there's no wiring at the door, we run new low-voltage cable from the transformer, usually through the basement or attic. For a doorbell that doesn't ring, we test the button, wiring, chime and transformer to find the failed part.

Part of a Bigger Setup

Doorbells often go with other upgrades: cameras, lighting and smart locks. See smart home wiring for the rest, and security and flood lighting for motion lights by the door. General low-voltage runs are covered under low voltage wiring.

What Affects the Cost

Installing on good existing wiring is quick. A new transformer, new wire runs or a doorbell at a new location takes more time.

Why does my Ring doorbell keep losing power?

Usually the transformer can't supply enough steady power. Upgrading it typically fixes the problem.

Where is my doorbell transformer?

Often near the panel, the furnace, in a closet or attached to a junction box in the basement. We'll find it.

Can I keep my old chime with a video doorbell?

Often yes, with the right adapter. Some digital chimes need to be replaced.
