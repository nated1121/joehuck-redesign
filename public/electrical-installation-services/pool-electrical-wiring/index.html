Pool Electrical Work We Handle

  • Circuits for pumps, filters, heaters and heat pumps
  • Pool and spa lighting, including low-voltage LED lights
  • Equipotential bonding of the pool shell, deck and metal parts
  • GFCI protection for pumps and receptacles
  • Replacing old equipment wiring that failed an inspection

Equipotential Bonding

Bonding connects the pool's reinforcing steel, metal parts, deck and nearby metal so they're all at the same electrical potential. That way, a stray voltage can't create a difference between the water and the deck that a swimmer would feel. It's required for in-ground and many above-ground pools and must be installed and inspected before concrete is poured, so we coordinate closely with your pool builder.

Equipment and Lighting

We run circuits to the equipment pad sized for the pump, filter, heater or heat pump, install a disconnect and GFCI protection where required, and make the connections. Pool lighting uses low-voltage LED fixtures and transformers or listed line-voltage fixtures installed to strict rules. Receptacles near the pool must be a set distance from the water and GFCI-protected; see outdoor outlet installation. For lighting around the pool and yard, see landscape lighting.

Hot Tubs and Spas

Spas attached to the pool are wired along with it. Standalone tubs have their own requirements; see hot tub electrical hookup.

What Affects the Cost

The equipment you're powering, the distance from the panel, bonding requirements and panel capacity all shape the price. We work from your pool builder's plans to give you a written estimate.

Working With Your Pool Builder

Pool electrical has to be coordinated at several stages. Bonding is installed and inspected before the concrete or deck is poured. Conduit for lights and equipment is set during excavation. Equipment is wired once the pad is ready, and final connections and testing happen before the pool is filled and started. We work from your builder's schedule and plans, and we're on site at each stage so nothing is missed. Above-ground pools need less work, but they still need bonding, a GFCI-protected pump circuit and a properly located outlet.

Replacing Old Pool Wiring

Older pools often have equipment wiring that's corroded, undersized or missing required bonding and GFCI protection. When you replace a pump or heater, it's a good time to bring the wiring up to date.

What is pool bonding?

Connecting all the metal in and around the pool so there's no voltage difference a swimmer could feel. It's required and inspected before concrete goes in.

Do pool pumps need GFCI protection?

Yes, current code requires GFCI protection for pool pump motors.

Can you replace my old pool equipment wiring?

Yes. We update circuits, disconnects and bonding when equipment is replaced.
