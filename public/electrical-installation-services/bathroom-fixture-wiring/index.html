Common Bathroom Electrical Projects

  • New vanity lighting or sconces beside the mirror
  • An exhaust fan that's missing, noisy or vents into the attic
  • Heated floors or a towel warmer
  • Recessed or shower lights rated for wet locations
  • Outlets without GFCI protection, or not enough of them

Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan clears moisture that otherwise feeds mold and peels paint. We install quiet, properly sized fans, and duct them to the outside, never just into the attic. A humidity-sensing fan or a timer switch keeps it running long enough to clear the room after a shower. Fans over a tub or shower need to be rated for the location and are often GFCI-protected.

Lighting in the Bath

Good bathroom lighting comes from the sides of the mirror, not only from above. We install wall sconces and vanity bars at the right height, and recessed lights rated for damp or wet locations over the tub and shower. Dimmers are a nice addition for night-time.

Outlets, Heated Floors and Protection

Bathroom outlets need GFCI protection and a 20 amp circuit, and code limits where outlets can go near tubs and showers. See GFCI outlet installation for details. Heated floor mats and towel warmers get their own GFCI-protected supply and a thermostat or timer. In a remodel, we coordinate with your tile installer so the heating mat is tested before and after tile goes down.

What Affects the Cost

A fixture swap is quick. Adding a vented fan, new circuits or heated floors takes longer, especially in finished second-floor baths. We plan the work around your remodel schedule.

Planning a Bathroom Remodel

A bathroom remodel is the best time to fix everything at once. Before the walls close, think about lighting on both sides of the mirror, a vented fan with a humidity control, an outlet inside the vanity or medicine cabinet for toothbrushes and razors, and whether you want heated floors or a towel warmer. We'll rough in all of it, pass the inspection before the tile and drywall go up, and return to trim it out when the finishes are done. Coordinating with your contractor keeps the project on schedule.

Good to Know.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Can my bathroom fan vent into the attic?

No. It should vent outdoors. Venting into the attic puts moisture where it can cause mold and rot.

How high should vanity lights be?

Sconces beside a mirror usually sit around eye level. We'll help you place them for your mirror and vanity.

Do bathroom outlets need GFCI?

Yes. All bathroom outlets need GFCI protection.
