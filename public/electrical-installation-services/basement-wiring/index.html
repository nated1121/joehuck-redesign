What a Finished Basement Usually Needs

  • Outlets along every wall, plus for the TV and bar area
  • Recessed lighting on dimmers, zoned by area
  • A dedicated circuit for the sump pump, freezer or gym
  • Smoke and CO alarms tied into your existing system
  • Room in the panel for all of it

Planning the Layout

We walk the space with you and your contractor and plan it room by room: outlets for furniture and TVs, lighting zones, a bathroom or bar, and a home office or gym. Code sets minimum outlet spacing and requires arc-fault protection for living areas and ground-fault protection for unfinished areas, bathrooms and wet bars. We plan for all of it up front.

Lighting a Basement

Basements are dark, and lighting makes or breaks the space. Recessed lights work well under low ceilings; we use slim LED fixtures that fit around ductwork and pipes and put them on dimmers by zone.

The Panel and Protected Circuits

Basement finishes add a lot of circuits, and many older main panels are full. A subpanel in the basement is often the simplest way to add space. Sump pumps and freezers get their own circuits so an unrelated trip can't take them down. New smoke and CO alarms are required and interconnected with the rest of the house.

What Affects the Cost

Square footage, the number of rooms, lighting count, bathrooms or bars and whether a subpanel is needed shape the price. We quote from your plan.

Damp Basements

If your basement gets damp, we'll address it in the plan. Outlets in unfinished areas need GFCI protection, fixtures in damp spots need the right rating, and wiring should stay clear of areas that take on water. Sump pumps get their own circuit, and we can add a battery backup outlet for them. We'll also make sure the panel and any subpanel stay accessible, with the clear working space in front of them that the code requires, even after the finish work is done.

Additions, Attics and Remodels

The same planning applies to any new living space: additions, attic conversions, sunrooms and major remodels. We lay out circuits, outlets, switches and lighting around how you'll use the room, coordinate with your builder, and handle the rough-in, trim-out and inspections.

New Construction

For new homes we also install the service and panel and provide temporary power during the build, working to your builder's schedule from rough-in to the final inspection.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Do I need a permit to finish my basement's electrical?

Yes. New circuits and wiring need a permit with rough and final inspections.

How many outlets does a basement need?

Code requires outlets along the walls so no point is more than six feet from one. We usually add more for TVs, desks and bars.

Can you wire around existing ductwork and pipes?

Yes. We use slim LED fixtures and plan routes around mechanicals.
