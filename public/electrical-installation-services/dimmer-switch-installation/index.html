Signs You Need a Better Dimmer

  • LED lights flicker or strobe at low settings
  • The dimmer or fixture buzzes
  • Lights don't dim all the way down, or turn off before the bottom
  • You switched to LED bulbs and the old dimmer stopped working right
  • You want to dim a room from two places

Dimmers for Fans and Special Fixtures

Ceiling fans need a fan-speed control, never a light dimmer, because a light dimmer can damage the motor. Low-voltage fixtures, some under-cabinet lights and LED strips need dimmers or drivers designed for their power supply. We'll match the control to each fixture, so everything works smoothly and lasts. If you have a mix of fixtures on one switch, we may recommend separating them so each can be controlled properly.

Why LEDs Need the Right Dimmer

Older dimmers were designed for incandescent bulbs, which draw a lot of power. LEDs draw very little, and many older dimmers can't control such a small load smoothly. The result is flicker, buzzing and a limited dimming range. We install dimmers rated for LED loads and adjust their low-end trim settings so the lights dim smoothly without dropping out. We also check that the bulbs or fixtures themselves are dimmable, because not every LED is.

Types of Dimmers We Install

We install standard slide, rotary and paddle dimmers, dimmers that work from more than one location, and smart dimmers from brands like Lutron that you can control from your phone or voice assistant. Smart dimmers usually need a neutral wire in the switch box, which some older homes don't have; we'll check and find a solution if yours doesn't. For whole-room scenes and app control, see smart lighting installation.

Pairing Dimmers With New Lighting

Dimmers matter most with recessed lighting, chandeliers and dining room fixtures. When we install new lighting, we match the dimmer to the fixtures from the start. Replacing an ordinary switch is covered under switch and outlet installation.

What Affects the Cost

Standard dimmers are inexpensive; smart and multi-location dimmers cost more. Boxes with several switches, missing neutrals and older wiring can add time.

Why do my LED lights flicker on the dimmer?

Usually the dimmer isn't designed for LED loads, or its low-end setting needs adjustment. The right dimmer fixes it in most cases.

Can any LED bulb be dimmed?

No. Check the package for "dimmable." Non-dimmable LEDs will flicker or fail on a dimmer.

Do smart dimmers need special wiring?

Most need a neutral wire in the switch box. We'll check yours and recommend a model that works.
