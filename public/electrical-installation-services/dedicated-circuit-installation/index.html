Signs You Need a Dedicated Circuit

  • A breaker trips when a particular appliance runs with anything else
  • Lights dim when the appliance starts up
  • You're adding a freezer, wine fridge or second refrigerator
  • You need a reliable circuit for a sump pump or medical equipment
  • You're setting up a home office, gym, workshop or window AC

What Gets a Dedicated Circuit

Code requires dedicated circuits for many built-in appliances, including dishwashers, disposals, built-in microwaves, and the washer and furnace. Beyond the rules, anything that draws a lot of power for a long time benefits from its own circuit: space heaters, window air conditioners, freezers, gym equipment and computer setups. Sump pumps and freezers get their own circuit so an unrelated trip doesn't leave you with a flooded basement or spoiled food.

120-Volt and 240-Volt Circuits

Most household equipment runs on 120-volt, 15 or 20 amp circuits. Larger equipment like ranges, dryers, water heaters, welders, air compressors, mini-splits and EV chargers needs a 240-volt circuit sized to the equipment. Our appliance wiring page covers those hookups, and EV charger installation has its own page.

How We Install It

We confirm your panel has space and capacity, size the wire and breaker for the load, and run the new circuit by the cleanest route, often from the basement or attic with a small opening for the new outlet. We add the ground-fault or arc-fault protection the location requires, label the breaker and test the circuit under load. If the panel is full or near capacity, we'll talk through a subpanel or panel upgrade.

What Affects the Cost

Distance from the panel, the circuit's size, how hard the route is, and the type of breaker required all shape the price. A new 20 amp outlet near the panel is a quick job; a 240-volt run to a far corner of the house takes longer.

Appliance Hookups

Ranges, cooktops, wall ovens, dryers, water heaters, dishwashers, disposals and built-in microwaves each need a circuit sized for that appliance and the right connection at the end of it. We wire new appliances, handle gas-to-electric and induction conversions, and check that the cord, receptacle and breaker all match the manufacturer's requirements.

Kitchen Remodels

A remodel is the best time to get kitchen wiring right. Code calls for at least two 20-amp countertop circuits, GFCI protection, and dedicated circuits for the dishwasher, disposal, microwave and refrigerator. We plan island power and range circuits alongside recessed and under-cabinet lighting, and work around your contractor's schedule.

Good to Know

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Does my refrigerator need its own circuit?

It's strongly recommended, and often required in new kitchens. A shared circuit can trip and leave your food warm.

Can a space heater run on a regular outlet?

It can, but a 1,500-watt heater uses most of a 15-amp circuit by itself. That's the classic cause of tripping in bedrooms and offices.

Do I need a permit for a new circuit?

Yes, a new circuit generally needs a permit. We handle it.
