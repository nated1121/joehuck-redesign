Signs Your Home Is Due for Upgrades

  • Two-prong outlets, or three-prong outlets with no ground behind them
  • Extension cords and power strips in most rooms
  • Breakers trip when you run a space heater, hair dryer or window unit
  • Not enough outlets in the kitchen, home office or bedrooms
  • Dim rooms with a single ceiling fixture or none at all

Upgrades We Do Most Often

We start by walking through the house with you and listing what bothers you. Then we recommend the upgrades that fix the biggest problems for the money. Common ones include adding circuits to split overloaded rooms, grounding outlets or protecting them with GFCIs, adding outlets where furniture actually goes, and upgrading switches to dimmers or smart controls.

For the heart of the house, see our kitchen electrical upgrade page. For wet areas, garages and outdoors, GFCI outlet installation is often the first and cheapest safety improvement. When the house needs more power overall, it starts with a panel upgrade.

Planning Upgrades in Phases

You don't have to do it all at once. We can put together a written plan, ordered by safety first and convenience second, and price each piece separately. Many customers do the safety items now and the lighting and convenience upgrades later. Anything we install now is planned so it won't have to be redone when the next phase comes.

What Affects the Cost

The number of circuits and outlets, how easy it is to reach the walls from the attic and basement, and plaster versus drywall all affect price. We quote each item, so you can choose.

Upgrades That Pay Off Quickly

Some upgrades cost little and make a noticeable difference: replacing worn outlets that don't grip plugs, adding a circuit for a home office, putting the porch and garage lights on a timer or smart switch, and adding outlets where you're using extension cords. We'll point these out during the walkthrough, so you can get quick wins while planning bigger projects.

Questions

Good to Know.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

Can I just swap two-prong outlets for three-prong ones?

Only if there's a ground wire, or if the outlet is protected by a GFCI and labeled "No equipment ground." We'll tell you which option is right for each circuit.

Where should I start if my budget is limited?

Safety first: GFCI protection in wet areas, working smoke alarms and fixing anything warm or overloaded. Convenience upgrades can follow.

Will upgrades increase my home's value?

Updated electrical systems help with inspections and insurance, and buyers notice modern outlets, lighting and panel capacity.
