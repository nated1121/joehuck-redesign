How We Work on New Construction

We start with the plans and a walkthrough. We'll suggest outlet, switch and lighting locations based on how the rooms will be used, not just the code minimums. Then we handle temporary power for the build if needed, the new service and panel, and the rough-in wiring once framing is done. We coordinate the rough inspection before insulation and drywall go up.

After finishes are in, we come back to trim out: devices, fixtures, appliance connections and final testing. Then we schedule the final inspection. Throughout, we work to your builder's schedule, show up when we say we will, and keep a clean site.

Planning Ahead

The cheapest time to add wiring is before the walls are closed. We'll talk through what to plan for now, even if you don't install it yet: EV charger circuits, smart home and network wiring, generator connections, outdoor outlets and landscape lighting feeds. For additions tied into an older house, we'll check whether your existing service can carry the new load or needs a panel upgrade. Our electrical wiring installation page covers smaller renovation wiring.

What Affects the Cost

Square footage, the number of circuits and fixtures, the size of the service, and specialty systems like lighting controls or low-voltage wiring all shape the price. We give builders and homeowners itemized proposals from the plans.

Lighting and Controls Worth Planning Now

New construction is the best time to plan lighting in layers: recessed lighting for general light, pendants and chandeliers as focal points, under-cabinet and accent lighting, and outdoor lighting for entries and landscape. Decide where you want dimmers, three-way switches at stairs and hallways, and smart controls, so the right wiring and boxes go in during rough-in. It costs very little to add a neutral wire to every switch box or blocking for a heavy fixture now, and much more to add it after the drywall is up. We'll walk the framed house with you before rough-in to confirm every location.
