Signs a Breaker Needs Replacing

  • It trips with a light load, or won't stay reset
  • The handle feels loose or mushy, or won't latch
  • The breaker or the panel cover is warm to the touch
  • You see scorch marks, melted plastic or smell burning near the panel
  • A breaker hums or buzzes

A Breaker That Never Trips

A breaker that never trips can be worse than one that trips too often. Some older breakers can fail in the on position and stop protecting the wire. We test suspect breakers and replace any that don't respond.

Why We Don't Just Swap It

A breaker that trips is usually doing its job. Before we replace one, we test the circuit for overloads, short circuits and ground faults. If the circuit simply has too much on it, a new breaker won't help, and we'll talk about adding a dedicated circuit instead. If there's a loose connection, a damaged wire or a failing device on the circuit, we fix that too. Our repair and troubleshooting page explains how we track those problems down.

Using the Right Breaker for Your Panel

Breakers have to be listed for the panel they go in. A breaker from another brand may fit in the slot and still make a poor connection to the bus bar, which causes heat and can damage the panel itself. We use breakers that match your panel's label. When a panel is old enough that parts are hard to find, or it's a brand with a known history of failures, we'll tell you straight whether it's time for a panel upgrade.

Bringing Breakers Up to Code

When we replace breakers on bedroom and living-area circuits, current code often requires arc-fault protection, and circuits in kitchens, bathrooms, laundry areas and garages need ground-fault protection. We'll explain what applies to your home and use AFCI breakers or GFCI protection where they're required or make sense.

What Affects the Cost

Standard breakers cost far less than AFCI, GFCI or dual-function breakers. The rest depends on what we find on the circuit and whether the panel's bus bar is damaged.

Can a circuit breaker go bad?

Yes. Breakers wear with age and repeated trips, and heat from a loose connection can damage them. A worn breaker may trip too easily or, worse, fail to trip at all.

Is it safe to replace a breaker myself?

We don't recommend it. The panel's main lugs stay live even with the main breaker off, and the wrong breaker can damage the panel.

Why does my breaker keep tripping?

Usually an overloaded circuit, a short, a ground fault or a failing breaker. We test to find which one before replacing anything.
