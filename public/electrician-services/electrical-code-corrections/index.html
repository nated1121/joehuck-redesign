Items We Fix Most Often

  • Double-tapped breakers and missing panel knockouts
  • Missing GFCI protection in kitchens, baths, garages, basements and outdoors
  • Three-prong outlets with no ground, or reversed polarity
  • Open junction boxes, loose cable and missing cover plates
  • Missing or expired smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
  • Amateur wiring from past projects

How It Works

Send us the electrical section of the inspection report, or the whole thing. We'll review it, visit to confirm each item, and give you a written price for each one. Some findings are quick fixes; others, like an outdated panel, need a real decision, and we'll lay out your options without pressure.

Most correction jobs take a day or less. When the work needs a permit, we pull it and handle the inspection. When we're done, you get an itemized invoice describing what was corrected, which most buyers and lenders accept as proof the items were addressed.

Common Fixes

Missing ground-fault protection is the most common finding, and GFCI outlet installation usually solves it quickly. Ungrounded three-prong outlets can be fixed with GFCI protection and labeling, or with ground wire installation. Pennsylvania requires carbon monoxide alarms in homes with fuel-burning appliances or attached garages when they're sold, and we install those along with smoke alarms.

Buyers and Landlords

If you're buying, we can review the report before your negotiation deadline and give you real prices to work with. Landlords use us to correct items flagged on rental inspections. For a broader look at an older house, see our electrical maintenance check.

Items That Come Up in Older Homes

Older homes around Bucks County tend to show the same findings: ungrounded outlets, missing GFCIs, a fuse box or an outdated panel, and wiring added by past owners without a permit. In homes built in the late 1960s and early 1970s, aluminum wiring sometimes appears on the report. None of these has to derail a sale. We'll explain which items are safety concerns, which are simply out of date, and what it costs to correct each one, so you can negotiate with real numbers.
