Signs Your Home Needs Rewiring

  • You have knob-and-tube wiring, or cloth-covered cable that crumbles when touched
  • Most outlets are two-prong, with no ground wire
  • Lights dim or flicker when appliances start
  • Outlets or switch plates feel warm
  • Your insurer won't cover the house until the wiring is replaced
  • You're remodeling and the walls are already open

How We Rewire a House

We start with a walkthrough to map what's in the walls and what you want the finished house to have: more outlets, dedicated kitchen circuits, grounded receptacles, better lighting. Then we plan cable routes through attics, basements and existing wall cavities. Most rewires need only small, neat access holes rather than open walls, and we can arrange drywall and plaster patching when we're done.

New wiring is grounded throughout and gets arc-fault and ground-fault protection where current code requires it. We usually rewire one area at a time, so you keep power in the rest of the house while we work. Many rewires include a panel upgrade, since an old fuse box or undersized panel can't support the new circuits.

Partial Rewiring

Not every house needs a full rewire. If the problem is a few ungrounded circuits, ground wire installation may be enough. If your house was built in the late 1960s or early 1970s, check whether it has aluminum branch wiring; our aluminum wiring page covers that separately.

What Affects the Cost

Square footage, number of stories, plaster versus drywall, attic and basement access, and how many circuits and outlets you want all shape the price. We give you a written estimate by room or area, so you can phase the work if that fits your budget better.

Living in the House During a Rewire

We plan the work so the kitchen, a bathroom and bedrooms stay usable each night. Furniture gets moved away from the walls we're working on and covered, and we clean up dust at the end of each day. You'll always know which rooms we're in next.

Good to Know.

Still have a question? Call 215-906-4634. You'll talk to an electrician, not a call center.

How long does it take to rewire a house?

A typical single-family home takes one to two weeks, depending on size and access. We work in sections so you're not without power the whole time.

Do I have to move out while you rewire?

Usually not. We work one area at a time and restore power at the end of each day.

Is knob-and-tube wiring dangerous?

It was well made, but after decades the insulation breaks down, it has no ground, and it was never meant to be buried in insulation. Most insurers now want it replaced.
