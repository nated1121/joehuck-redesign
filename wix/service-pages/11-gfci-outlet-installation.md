**Section 1**

- **H2:** GFCI Outlets vs. GFCI Breakers
- Paragraph:

> There are two ways to protect a circuit. A GFCI outlet protects itself and every outlet wired after it on the circuit. A GFCI breaker in the panel protects the whole circuit from its source, which is useful when outlets are hard to reach, when a circuit feeds outdoor outlets, or for equipment like hot tubs. We'll choose the right method for each location, and label protected outlets downstream so you know where to reset.


**Section 2**

- **H2:** Installing and Testing
- Paragraph:

> We identify the first outlet on each circuit, install GFCI protection there or at the panel, and test every outlet it covers. GFCIs can also protect ungrounded three-prong outlets when a ground wire isn't available, labeled "No equipment ground" as the code requires; see ground wire installation for a permanent ground. For outdoor locations, see outdoor outlet installation. In bathrooms, we often pair GFCI work with bathroom fixture wiring.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"ground wire installation"** → Home Rewiring page
  - Link **"outdoor outlet installation"** → GFCI Outlet Installation page
  - Link **"bathroom fixture wiring"** → Ceiling Fan Installation page

**Section 3**

- **H2:** GFCIs That Trip or Won't Reset
- Paragraph:

> A GFCI that trips often may have found a real problem, such as moisture in an outdoor box, a worn appliance or damaged wiring. A GFCI that won't reset may have failed, or may have no power coming in. We find the cause. GFCIs also wear out and should be tested monthly with the test button.


**Section 4**

- **H2:** What Affects the Cost
- Paragraph:

> GFCI outlets are affordable; GFCI breakers cost more. Most homes need only a handful, placed at the start of each circuit.


**Section 5**

- **H2:** Outdoor Outlets
- Paragraph:

> Every outdoor outlet needs GFCI protection, a weather-resistant receptacle and an in-use cover that stays closed while something is plugged in. We add outlets for decks, patios and porches, holiday lights, landscape lighting transformers, pool equipment and yard tools, and replace cracked or rusted ones. An outdoor GFCI that trips every time it rains usually has water getting past a worn cover or a damaged receptacle, and we fix the cause instead of just resetting it.


**Section 6**

- **H2:** GFCI Work Around Yardley
- Paragraph:

> We add GFCI protection as part of code corrections before sales in Southampton and Langhorne, and in basements and garages in Feasterville.

- **H3:** Why did several outlets stop working at once?

> Often a GFCI in another room, the garage or outside has tripped. Press its reset button. If it won't reset, call us.

- **H3:** How often should I test my GFCIs?

> Once a month. Press test, confirm power goes off, then press reset.

- **H3:** Do GFCI outlets wear out?

> Yes. Most last many years, but a GFCI that no longer trips when tested should be replaced.
