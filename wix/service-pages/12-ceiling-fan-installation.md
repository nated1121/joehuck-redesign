**Signs box**

- **H2:** When to Call an Electrician for a Fan
- Bulleted list:

> - You're replacing a light fixture with a fan
> - The room has no ceiling fixture, only a switched outlet
> - You want the fan and light on separate switches
> - The fan wobbles, hums or the light flickers
> - The ceiling is high or sloped

**Section 1**

- **H2:** Fan-Rated Boxes Matter
- Paragraph:

> A ceiling fan is heavy and it moves, and a standard light fixture box isn't built to hold it. Every fan we install is mounted to a fan-rated box or brace secured to the ceiling framing. When we replace a light with a fan, we check the existing box and replace it if it isn't rated, usually without opening the ceiling. That's the difference between a fan that runs quietly for years and one that wobbles or works loose.


**Section 2**

- **H2:** Adding a Fan Where There's No Fixture
- Paragraph:

> Many older homes, especially 1950s homes in lower Bucks County, have bedrooms with no ceiling fixture. We run a new switch leg from the wall switch to the ceiling through the attic, install a fan-rated box and wire the fan. If you want the fan and light on separate wall switches, we run the extra conductor while we're there. Remote and wall-control kits are an option when adding wire isn't practical. For light-only fixtures, see ceiling light installation.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"ceiling light installation"** → Light Fixture Installation page

**Section 3**

- **H2:** Assembly, Balance and Setup
- Paragraph:

> We assemble the fan, set the blade pitch, balance it so it runs without wobble and set the direction for the season. On high or sloped ceilings, we install the right downrod length and slope adapter. If you're ventilating a hot attic rather than cooling a room, see attic fan installation.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"attic fan installation"** → Ceiling Fan Installation page

**Section 4**

- **H2:** What Affects the Cost
- Paragraph:

> Replacing a fixture with a fan is the quickest job. Adding a new switch leg, separate fan and light control, high ceilings and heavy fans take more time. We quote the whole job, including the box.


**Section 5**

- **H2:** Porch and Outdoor Fans
- Paragraph:

> Covered porches and patios need fans rated for damp or wet locations, and they're usually mounted to boxes that can handle outdoor conditions. We'll check the rating and wire them with GFCI protection where required.


**Section 6**

- **H2:** Attic Fans
- Paragraph:

> A powered attic ventilator pulls hot, humid air out of the attic in summer. We install gable fans, wire roof-mounted units, add thermostat and humidistat controls, and repair attic fans that have stopped running.


**Section 7**

- **H2:** Bathroom Exhaust Fans and Fixtures
- Paragraph:

> Bathroom fans need to vent outside, not into the attic, and work best on a timer or humidity sensor. During bathroom updates we also wire vanity lights, wet-rated shower lights, heated floors, towel warmers and the GFCI protection every bathroom outlet needs.


**Section 8**

- **H2:** Ceiling Fans Around Yardley
- Paragraph:

> We install a lot of ceiling fans in the original bedrooms of Fairless Hills, Levittown and Feasterville homes, and on porches in Southampton.

- **H3:** Can I put a ceiling fan where my light fixture is?

> Usually, if the box is fan-rated or can be replaced with one. We check and upgrade the box as part of the install.

- **H3:** Can the fan and light be on separate switches?

> Yes, if there's an extra conductor, which we can run. Otherwise a remote or smart control is an option.

- **H3:** Why does my fan wobble?

> Usually unbalanced blades or a loose mount. We balance the fan and check the box.
