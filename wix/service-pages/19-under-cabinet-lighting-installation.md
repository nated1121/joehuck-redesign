**Section 1**

- **H2:** Choosing the Fixtures
- Paragraph:

> Slim LED light bars give even light with no hot spots and install neatly at the front of the cabinet. LED strip lighting in an aluminum channel with a diffuser works well for a continuous line. Old-style puck lights create pools of light and are best for accents. We'll help you choose the color temperature to match your other lights and the brightness for your counters.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"LED strip lighting"** → Under Cabinet Lighting Installation page

**Section 2**

- **H2:** Hardwired and Hidden
- Paragraph:

> We run power from a nearby circuit into the cabinets and to a wall switch, often fishing the wire behind the backsplash or through the cabinets. Low-voltage systems need a driver, which we hide in a cabinet where it can still be reached. Everything goes on a switch or dimmer, sometimes with the pendants or on a separate control. In a remodel, we rough in the wiring before the backsplash goes up.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"dimmer"** → Switch & Outlet Installation page

**Section 3**

- **H2:** Part of a Kitchen Lighting Plan
- Paragraph:

> Under-cabinet lighting works best as one layer of a plan: recessed lights for general light, pendants over the island, and under-cabinet lights for tasks. See kitchen electrical upgrades for circuits, outlets and appliances.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"pendants"** → Light Fixture Installation page
  - Link **"kitchen electrical upgrades"** → Dedicated Circuit Installation page

**Section 4**

- **H2:** What Affects the Cost
- Paragraph:

> Linear feet of cabinets, fixture type, whether walls and backsplash are finished and where the switch goes all shape the price.


**Section 5**

- **H2:** Placement and Color
- Paragraph:

> Mounting fixtures toward the front edge of the cabinet lights the counter rather than the backsplash, and reduces reflections on shiny counters. Warm or neutral color temperatures usually match the kitchen's other lighting best. If your counters are polished stone, we'll choose fixtures with diffused lenses so you don't see a row of reflected dots. For glass-front cabinets, a separate strip inside the cabinet shows off what's on display. We can put those on their own switch or dimmer.


**Section 6**

- **H2:** LED Strip Lighting
- Paragraph:

> Hardwired LED strip lighting in aluminum channels with diffusers gives an even line of light with no visible dots. We use it in coves, cabinets, shelves, stairs, bars and toe-kicks, with drivers sized for the run and tucked out of sight.


**Section 7**

- **H2:** Cove and Accent Lighting
- Paragraph:

> Soft light in a tray ceiling or along crown molding, or accent light on shelves, artwork and built-ins, makes a room feel finished. We hide the drivers and put everything on a dimmer.

- **H3:** Can under-cabinet lights be added to an existing kitchen?

> Yes. We fish power into the cabinets and add a switch, usually without disturbing the backsplash much.

- **H3:** Hardwired or plug-in?

> Hardwired is cleaner, runs on a wall switch and doesn't use up your counter outlets.

- **H3:** Can they be dimmed?

> Yes, with dimmable fixtures and a compatible dimmer or driver.

- **H3:** How long do LED under-cabinet lights last?

> Quality LED fixtures typically last many years of normal use. Keeping the driver accessible makes it easy to replace if it ever fails.
