## Strip 3: Article

**Photo:** `site/assets/img/jobs/pool-timer-outdoor-outlet.jpg`  
**Alt text:** Testing a pool pump timer and weatherproof outdoor outlet on a post

**Signs box**

- **H2:** What Your Hot Tub Needs
- Bulleted list:

> - A 240-volt circuit sized to the tub, commonly 40, 50 or 60 amps
> - A GFCI-protected disconnect within sight of the tub and at least five feet away
> - Bonding of the tub's metal parts and nearby metal
> - Space and capacity in your panel
> - A permit and inspection

**Section 1**

- **H2:** Planning the Location
- Paragraph:

> Before the tub arrives, we look at where it will sit, where the panel is and how the circuit will get there. Most runs go underground in conduit or along the house exterior. The disconnect has to be within sight of the tub but at least five feet from its inside wall, so someone in the water can't reach it. We also check overhead lines, since hot tubs have required clearances from them.


**Section 2**

- **H2:** Installation
- Paragraph:

> We run the circuit from the panel with wire sized for the tub's amperage and the distance, install the GFCI disconnect, and connect the tub according to the manufacturer's instructions. The tub's metal parts are bonded as the code requires. After the inspection, we power it up with you and confirm the GFCI trips when tested. If your panel lacks capacity or space, we'll explain options including a panel upgrade.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"panel upgrade"** → Electrical Panel Upgrade page

**Section 3**

- **H2:** Plug-In Tubs and Swim Spas
- Paragraph:

> Some small "plug and play" tubs run on a 120-volt outlet, but they still need a dedicated, GFCI-protected outdoor outlet in the right place. Swim spas usually need larger circuits and sometimes two. For in-ground pools, see pool electrical wiring.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"outdoor outlet"** → GFCI Outlet Installation page
  - Link **"pool electrical wiring"** → Hot Tub Electrical Hookup page

**Section 4**

- **H2:** What Affects the Cost
- Paragraph:

> The distance from the panel, the tub's amperage, trenching or surface routing, and panel capacity drive the price. We'll give you a written estimate before delivery day.


**Section 5**

- **H2:** Before Delivery Day
- Paragraph:

> Order the tub, then call us with the model. We'll confirm its electrical requirements from the manufacturer's manual, plan the circuit and disconnect location, pull the permit and schedule the work so the wiring is inspected before the tub arrives. That way it can be filled and running the day it's set. If the tub is going on a deck, we'll plan the route with your deck builder.


**Section 6**

- **H2:** Pool Wiring
- Paragraph:

> In-ground and above-ground pools need circuits for the pump, filter and heater, GFCI protection, pool lighting and equipotential bonding, which ties the pool's metal parts together so there's no shock hazard in the water. Pools are inspected more closely than almost anything else we wire, and we handle the permit and the inspection.


**Section 7**

- **H2:** Hot Tubs Around Yardley
- Paragraph:

> We wire hot tubs and pools on decks and patios throughout Newtown and Warminster, and on larger properties around Washington Crossing.

  - Link **"Newtown"** → Newtown page
  - Link **"Warminster"** → Warminster page
