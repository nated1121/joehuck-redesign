## Strip 3: Article

**Photo:** `site/assets/img/jobs/kitchen-outlet-install.jpg`  
**Alt text:** Installing a new outlet on a kitchen backsplash

**Signs box**

- **H2:** Signs It's Time to Replace or Add
- Bulleted list:

> - Plugs fall out of the outlet or feel loose
> - A switch or outlet is cracked, warm or discolored
> - A switch crackles, sparks or has to be jiggled to work
> - You're running extension cords to reach an outlet
> - Outlets are two-prong or painted over
> - You want USB or USB-C charging built into the wall

**Section 1**

- **H2:** Replacing Worn Switches and Outlets
- Paragraph:

> When we replace a device, we don't just swap it. We check the box, the wire and the connections. Many older outlets were wired with push-in "backstab" connections that loosen over time and cause flickering, intermittent power and heat. We move those connections to the screw terminals or use quality back-wire devices, and replace any damaged wire ends. New outlets in homes are tamper-resistant, which keeps kids from pushing objects into the slots.

- Paragraph:

> If a switch is warm or flickers, that's a sign of a loose connection or a device that's failing, and it should be fixed soon. See electrical repair and troubleshooting if the problem is on more than one device.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"electrical repair and troubleshooting"** → Electrical Repair & Troubleshooting page

**Section 2**

- **H2:** Adding New Outlets and Switches
- Paragraph:

> We add outlets behind TVs and beds, along kitchen counters, in hallways, garages and home offices, usually by fishing wire from the basement or attic and cutting only a small opening for the new box. We can add switches for lights that only have a pull chain, and install combination switch-and-outlet devices where space is tight. Moving an existing outlet or switch is covered on our switch and outlet relocation page. For dimmers, see dimmer switch installation.

- Links in this section:
  - Link **"switch and outlet relocation"** → Switch & Outlet Installation page
  - Link **"dimmer switch installation"** → Switch & Outlet Installation page

**Section 3**

- **H2:** What Affects the Cost
- Paragraph:

> Replacing devices is priced per device and goes quickly when several are done at once. New outlets depend on access and distance from a circuit with capacity.


**Section 4**

- **H2:** Moving Outlets and Switches
- Paragraph:

> Outlets end up in the wrong place: behind a new wall-mounted TV, under new cabinets, or on the hinge side of a door. We move them where they make sense, close up the old boxes to code and keep wall repairs as small as possible.


**Section 5**

- **H2:** Dimmer Switches
- Paragraph:

> Most flicker and buzz problems with LED bulbs come from a dimmer that wasn't made for them. We install LED-compatible and smart dimmers matched to your fixtures, including dimming from more than one location.


**Section 6**

- **H2:** 3-Way and 4-Way Switches
- Paragraph:

> Want to control a light from the top and bottom of the stairs, or from both ends of a hallway? We add new switch locations and fix 3-way circuits that only work from one side, usually without opening more than the switch boxes.


**Section 7**

- **H2:** Outlet and Switch Work Around Yardley

- **H3:** Why do my plugs fall out of the outlet?

> The outlet's internal contacts are worn. It should be replaced, since a loose connection can overheat.

- **H3:** Are USB outlets worth it?

> For bedsides, kitchens and desks, yes. They free up plug space and charge devices without adapters.

- **H3:** Can I replace a switch myself?

> Many people do, but if the switch was warm or sparking, have the box and wiring checked, because the problem may be behind the switch.
