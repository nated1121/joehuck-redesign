## What a Panel Upgrade Involves

Most upgrades in older Yardley homes go from 100 amps to 200 amps. That usually means a new panel, new service entrance cable, a new meter base when the utility requires it, and updated grounding and bonding. We pull the permit with your township, schedule the disconnect and reconnect with PECO, and book the inspection.

On the day of the swap, your power is off for part of the day. We move every circuit to the new panel, replace any breakers that are damaged or wrong for the wire size, and add the protection current code requires. Before we leave, every breaker gets a clear, typed label, so you know what each one controls.

Sometimes a full upgrade isn't needed. If your service is big enough but the panel is out of space, a [subpanel](@electrical-subpanel-installation) may solve the problem for less. If you still have fuses, see our page on [fuse box replacement](@fuse-box-replacement). We'll tell you which option actually fits your house.

## What Affects the Cost

The price depends on the size of the new service, whether the meter base and service cable need replacing, where the panel sits in the house, and how many circuits need new breakers or repairs. Moving the panel to a new location costs more than a same-spot swap. Your written estimate spells out all of it before any work starts, so there are no surprises on the invoice.

## Breaker Replacement

A breaker that trips for no reason, feels hot or won't reset is telling you something. We find out why it failed first, whether that's a loose connection, an overloaded circuit or a breaker that's simply worn out, then replace it with the breaker your panel is listed for, not just one that fits. Mismatched breakers are one of the most common problems we find in older Bucks County panels.

## AFCI Breakers

Arc-fault (AFCI) breakers detect the kind of sparking that starts fires inside walls. Current code requires them on most living-space circuits, so we add them during panel upgrades and remodels. If an AFCI keeps tripping, there's usually a real cause, like a damaged cable or a loose connection, and we track it down instead of swapping in a standard breaker.

We also upgrade panels in the historic homes of [Doylestown](@doylestown), and coordinate with the borough utility on upgrades in [Quakertown](@quakertown).

Q: Do I need a permit to replace my electrical panel?

:::signs
## When a Subpanel Makes Sense
- Your main panel has no open slots, but your service isn't overloaded
- You're finishing a basement and need several new circuits
- A detached garage or workshop needs lights, outlets and tools
- An addition is a long run from the main panel
- You want space for a future EV charger or generator hookup

## How We Install a Subpanel

We start with a load calculation. A subpanel adds space, not capacity, so if your service is already close to its limit, the honest answer may be a [panel upgrade](@electrical-panel-upgrade) instead. When a subpanel fits, we choose its location, size the feeder cable and breaker to match what you'll run on it, and install it with separate ground and neutral bars as the code requires.

For a detached building, the feeder runs underground in conduit at the proper depth, and the building gets its own grounding electrode and a disconnect. Inside the house, we route cable through joists and finished spaces as cleanly as we can and patch only what we have to open. Every new circuit is labeled, and the job is permitted and inspected.

## What Affects the Cost

Cost depends mostly on the distance between the main panel and the subpanel, the size of the feeder, whether trenching is involved, and how many circuits you want installed now. A 60-amp subpanel in a basement next to the main panel is a very different job from a 100-amp feed to a barn across the yard. We price it out in writing before we start.

## Subpanels and Generators

A subpanel can also make a backup generator simpler. Instead of wiring a transfer switch to every circuit in the house, we can move the essentials, like the furnace, refrigerator, sump pump and a few lights and outlets, to a dedicated subpanel that the generator feeds. It's a common setup for homes that lose power in storms.

## Subpanels Around Yardley

We install subpanels for basement finishes in [Southampton](@southampton) and [Langhorne](@langhorne), and for detached garages and workshops in [Warminster](@warminster) and on the larger lots around [Washington Crossing](@washington-crossing). If you're adding a building, our [shed and garage wiring](@shed-garage-wiring) page covers the rest of that job.

## FAQ
Q: Will a subpanel give me more power?
A: No. It gives you more breaker space. The total power available is still limited by your main service, which is why we run a load calculation before recommending one.
Q: Can a subpanel go in a detached garage?
A: Yes. A detached building needs its own grounding and a disconnect, and the feeder runs underground in conduit. We handle all of it, including the permit.
Q: How big should my subpanel be?
A: It depends on what you plan to run. A basement might need 60 amps, while a workshop with welders or a garage with an EV charger may need 100 amps or more.

## Changing a Blown Fuse the Right Way

A fuse blows to protect the wire behind it. The most dangerous thing we find in older homes is a 20 or 30 amp fuse screwed into a socket meant for 15 amps, because the wire can overheat long before that fuse blows. When we change fuses, we match each one to its wire size and can install tamper-resistant Type S adapters so the wrong size can't go back in. If fuses keep blowing, that's a sign the circuit is overloaded, and the lasting fix is more circuits or a new panel.

## Replacing a Fuse Box With a Breaker Panel

Most fuse boxes we replace are on 60 amp services, so the job is usually a full [panel and service upgrade](@electrical-panel-upgrade) to 100 or 200 amps. We pull the permit, coordinate the PECO disconnect, install the new panel and service equipment, move each circuit to a properly sized breaker, and book the inspection. Homes with fuse boxes often have old wiring too, and we'll tell you honestly whether any of it needs [rewiring](@home-rewiring).

## What Affects the Cost

A fuse box swap costs more than a breaker-panel swap because the service equipment usually has to change too. The size of the new service, the condition of the existing wiring and the number of circuits all affect the price. Your written estimate covers everything.

## What to Expect on Replacement Day

Your power will be off for most of the day while we swap the equipment. We'll tell you in advance, so you can plan around refrigerators, medical devices and work from home, and we restore power as soon as the utility reconnects.

## Fuse Boxes Around Yardley

We still find fuse boxes in older homes along Radcliffe Street in [Bristol](@bristol), in [Morrisville](@morrisville) twins and rowhomes, and in older borough homes in [Sellersville](@sellersville) and [Perkasie](@perkasie).

## FAQ
Q: Is it safe to keep my fuse box?
A: A fuse box with correctly sized fuses isn't dangerous by itself. The problems are oversized fuses, overloaded circuits and old wiring, which is why we inspect it before recommending anything.
Q: Will my insurance company care about my fuse box?
A: Many insurers charge more for homes with fuse boxes or won't write a policy at all. Replacing it usually solves that problem.
Q: Can you just change a blown fuse?
A: Yes. We'll replace it with the correct size and check why it blew, so you know whether the circuit needs more work.

Some homes built in the mid-1960s to mid-1970s were wired with single-strand aluminum branch circuits. The wire itself isn't the problem; the connections are. Aluminum expands, contracts and oxidizes at outlets and switches, which can make them overheat. We repair and replace aluminum wiring in Yardley-area homes with methods the industry recognizes as safe.

:::signs
## Signs of an Aluminum Wiring Problem
- Your home was built between roughly 1965 and 1975
- The cable jacket is stamped "AL" or "Aluminum," or the wire is silver-colored
- Outlets or switch plates feel warm
- Lights flicker, or outlets work intermittently
- You smell burning plastic near an outlet or switch
- A home inspection flagged aluminum branch wiring
:::

## Repair or Replace?

There are two lasting fixes. The first is to keep the aluminum wiring and make every connection safe by attaching a short copper pigtail with a connector made for the job, such as AlumiConn or COPALUM. Every outlet, switch, light, junction box and panel connection in the house gets treated, not just the ones that look bad. The second is a full [rewire](@home-rewiring) with copper, which costs more but removes the aluminum completely.

We'll inspect your system and explain both options. What we won't do is use standard twist-on connectors or "CO/ALR" devices as a whole-house fix; they aren't a substitute for proper remediation.

## Our Remediation Process

We work circuit by circuit, opening every box, checking the wire for damage from past overheating, and installing the approved connectors with the right tools. Damaged devices get replaced, and outlets and switches are [replaced with new ones](@switch-outlet-installation) where needed. When we're done, you get documentation of the work, which buyers, inspectors and insurers often ask for.

## What Affects the Cost

The number of connection points drives the price: every outlet, switch, fixture and junction box counts. A full copper rewire depends on the house's size and access. We'll give you written prices for both approaches.

## Why Standard Connectors Aren't Enough

Aluminum and copper expand at different rates, and aluminum forms an oxide layer that doesn't conduct well. Ordinary twist-on connectors and the screw terminals on standard devices can loosen and heat up as they cycle. The connectors we use are designed and tested for aluminum-to-copper connections and are installed with the correct tools, which is why they're recognized as a permanent repair.

A: Some insurers won't, or require proof of remediation. Our documentation of the repair usually satisfies them.

## Where Surges Come From

Lightning gets the attention, but most surges start closer to home: utility switching, tree limbs on lines, and large motors in your own house turning on and off. Each surge wears on electronic circuit boards a little, which is why appliances sometimes die early for no clear reason. A panel-mounted protector diverts that excess voltage to ground before it reaches your circuits.

## How We Install It

We install a surge protective device in or beside your panel on a dedicated breaker, keeping the leads as short as possible so it reacts fast. We check your grounding and bonding while we're there, because a surge protector is only as good as the ground it dumps energy into. If your grounding needs work, see our page on [ground wire installation and repair](@ground-wire-installation-repair).

Newer editions of the electrical code require surge protection whenever a service is upgraded or replaced, so we include it in every [panel upgrade](@electrical-panel-upgrade). For the most protection, we recommend pairing the whole-house device with quality plug-in protectors for computers and home theaters.

## What Affects the Cost

The protector's rating, your panel type and whether a breaker space is available all affect the price. Most installs take about an hour.

## Signs Your Home Has Been Hit by Surges

Surge damage isn't always dramatic. Watch for electronics that fail after storms, garage door openers and furnace control boards that need replacing more often than they should, and LED bulbs that burn out early. Those are common signs of repeated small surges. A whole-house protector won't repair damage that's already happened, but it can prevent the next round.

## Surge Protection Around Yardley

Homes near the river in [New Hope](@new-hope) and [Washington Crossing](@washington-crossing), and anywhere with overhead lines through the trees, see more than their share of storm surges. We also add protection during panel upgrades in [Chalfont](@chalfont).

## FAQ
Q: Does a whole-house surge protector stop lightning?
A: Nothing stops a direct lightning strike. A surge protector handles the far more common surges from nearby strikes, the utility and your own appliances.
Q: How long does a surge protector last?
A: Most last many years, but they wear with each surge they absorb. Many models have an indicator light that shows when they need replacing.
Q: Do I still need power strips?
A: For computers and home theaters, a good plug-in protector adds a second layer. The whole-house unit protects everything else.

## FAQ
Q: How often should my home's electrical system be inspected?
A: For most homes, every few years, and any time you buy a house, add major loads or notice a problem.
Q: What's the most common thing you find?
A: GFCI outlets that no longer trip when tested, smoke alarms past their ten-year life, and loose connections in the panel.
Q: Is this the same as a home inspection?
A: No. A home inspector looks at the whole house; we go deeper into the electrical system and can fix what we find.
