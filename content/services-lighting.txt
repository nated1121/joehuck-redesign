Recessed lighting is the most requested lighting upgrade we do in Yardley. Done well, it makes a room look bigger, brighter and cleaner. Done badly, it looks like a runway. We plan the layout, pick the right fixtures and install them cleanly in finished ceilings.

:::signs
## Good Rooms for Recessed Lighting
- Kitchens that rely on one center fixture
- Living and family rooms without overhead lighting
- Basements with low ceilings
- Hallways and stairwells
- Bathrooms and showers (with wet-rated fixtures)
:::

## Planning the Layout

Good recessed lighting starts on paper. We space fixtures based on ceiling height and the room's use, keep them the right distance from walls so they wash the walls instead of leaving scallops, and line them up with cabinets, islands and furniture. We'll suggest color temperature (warmer for living spaces, a little cooler for kitchens and work areas) and zone the lights so they're controlled in useful groups.

## Fixtures That Fit Your Ceiling

Modern slim LED "wafer" lights need very little ceiling depth, which makes them ideal for basements and for ceilings with joists or ductwork in the way. Where there's attic insulation above, fixtures must be IC-rated for contact with insulation and airtight so they don't leak heated or cooled air. Over tubs and showers, we use fixtures rated for wet locations.

## Clean Installation in Finished Ceilings

Most of our recessed lighting jobs are in finished rooms. We cut the holes precisely, fish wire between fixtures through the ceiling from the attic or from below, and leave little or no patching. Every layout goes on a [dimmer](@dimmer-switch-installation) matched to the fixtures, so the lights dim smoothly without flicker. If you already have old cans, see [LED lighting retrofits](@led-lighting-retrofits). For a single decorative fixture, see [ceiling light installation](@ceiling-light-installation).

## What Affects the Cost

The number of lights, ceiling access, the type of fixture and how many switching zones you want drive the price. A kitchen might need 6 to 10 lights; a large basement more.

## Retrofit vs. New Construction Fixtures

In existing ceilings, we use remodel-style fixtures that install through the hole without access from above. In new construction or open ceilings, new-construction housings attach to the framing. Both look the same when finished; the choice depends on your ceiling.

## Recessed Lighting Around Yardley

We install recessed lighting in kitchens and family rooms throughout [Bensalem](@bensalem) and [Newtown](@newtown), and in basement finishes in [Southampton](@southampton).

## FAQ
Q: How many recessed lights do I need?
A: It depends on the room size, ceiling height and use. As a starting point, many rooms space lights roughly four to six feet apart. We lay it out for your room.
Q: Can you install recessed lights without an attic above?
A: Yes. Slim LED wafer lights and careful wire fishing let us install them in first-floor and basement ceilings.
Q: What color temperature should I choose?
A: Around 2700K to 3000K feels warm and suits living spaces. 3500K to 4000K is crisper for kitchens and work areas.
