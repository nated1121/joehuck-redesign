Recessed lighting is the most requested lighting upgrade we do in Yardley. Done well, it makes a room look bigger, brighter and cleaner. Done badly, it looks like a runway. We plan the layout, pick the right fixtures and install them cleanly in finished ceilings.

:::signs
## Good Rooms for Recessed Lighting
- Kitchens that rely on one center fixture
- Living and family rooms without overhead lighting
- Basements with low ceilings
- Hallways and stairwells
- Bathrooms and showers (with wet-rated fixtures)
:::

## Planning the Layout

Good recessed lighting starts on paper. We space fixtures based on ceiling height and the room's use, keep them the right distance from walls so they wash the walls instead of leaving scallops, and line them up with cabinets, islands and furniture. We'll suggest color temperature (warmer for living spaces, a little cooler for kitchens and work areas) and zone the lights so they're controlled in useful groups.

## Fixtures That Fit Your Ceiling

Modern slim LED "wafer" lights need very little ceiling depth, which makes them ideal for basements and for ceilings with joists or ductwork in the way. Where there's attic insulation above, fixtures must be IC-rated for contact with insulation and airtight so they don't leak heated or cooled air. Over tubs and showers, we use fixtures rated for wet locations.

## Clean Installation in Finished Ceilings

Most of our recessed lighting jobs are in finished rooms. We cut the holes precisely, fish wire between fixtures through the ceiling from the attic or from below, and leave little or no patching. Every layout goes on a [dimmer](@dimmer-switch-installation) matched to the fixtures, so the lights dim smoothly without flicker. If you already have old cans, see [LED lighting retrofits](@led-lighting-retrofits). For a single decorative fixture, see [ceiling light installation](@ceiling-light-installation).

## What Affects the Cost

The number of lights, ceiling access, the type of fixture and how many switching zones you want drive the price. A kitchen might need 6 to 10 lights; a large basement more.

## Retrofit vs. New Construction Fixtures

In existing ceilings, we use remodel-style fixtures that install through the hole without access from above. In new construction or open ceilings, new-construction housings attach to the framing. Both look the same when finished; the choice depends on your ceiling.

## Recessed Lighting Around Yardley

We install recessed lighting in kitchens and family rooms throughout [Bensalem](@bensalem) and [Newtown](@newtown), and in basement finishes in [Southampton](@southampton).

## FAQ
Q: How many recessed lights do I need?
A: It depends on the room size, ceiling height and use. As a starting point, many rooms space lights roughly four to six feet apart. We lay it out for your room.
Q: Can you install recessed lights without an attic above?
A: Yes. Slim LED wafer lights and careful wire fishing let us install them in first-floor and basement ceilings.
Q: What color temperature should I choose?
A: Around 2700K to 3000K feels warm and suits living spaces. 3500K to 4000K is crisper for kitchens and work areas.

A new light fixture is one of the fastest ways to change how a room looks. We install every kind of fixture in Yardley-area homes, from a simple flush mount to a two-story foyer chandelier, and we make sure the box, the support and the wiring behind it are right.

- The new fixture is heavier than the one it replaces
- A room has only a switched outlet and no ceiling light
- You want a light where there's no box, like over an island or beside a mirror
- The existing box is loose, or the wires are brittle or scorched
- The fixture goes on a high or sloped ceiling
:::

## Ceiling Lights

We remove the old fixture, check the box and wiring, and fix what isn't right: a loose or undersized box, wire insulation made brittle by years of heat, or a missing ground. Many older bedrooms and living rooms have a switched outlet instead of a ceiling light, so we run a new switch leg through the attic or from below and install a properly supported box. Replacing every builder-grade fixture in one visit is efficient and gives the house a consistent look.

## Chandeliers

A chandelier needs a box and support rated for its weight, and it has to hang at the right height: about 30 to 36 inches above a dining table, and high enough to walk under in a foyer. We assemble it, hang it level and put it on a dimmer. For two-story foyers, we bring the ladders and staging to do it safely.

## Pendant Lights

Pendants over a kitchen island or peninsula are spaced evenly along its length and hung so they light the counter without blocking the view across the room. We add boxes where they're needed, use adapters for sloped ceilings and put the pendants on their own switch or dimmer.

## Wall Sconces

Sconces beside a bathroom mirror light your face evenly, without the shadows an overhead light casts. We also install them along hallways and stairs, beside the bed and at exterior doors, fishing new boxes into finished walls at the right height.

## Track Lighting

Modern LED track and monorail lighting lets you aim light at artwork, a kitchen work area or a display, and move the heads later. It works well on sloped ceilings, where recessed lights can be hard to fit.

## What Affects the Cost

Straight swaps are quick, especially several in one visit. New boxes and switch legs, high ceilings, heavy fixtures and chandeliers that need a lot of assembly take longer. Your written estimate covers all of it before any work starts.

## Light Fixtures Around Yardley

We replace dated fixtures in older homes in [Levittown](@levittown) and [Bristol](@bristol), and hang chandeliers and pendants in remodeled kitchens and foyers in [Newtown](@newtown) and [Doylestown](@doylestown).

## FAQ
Q: Can I replace a light fixture myself?
A: Many people do simple swaps. If the box is loose, the wires are brittle or the fixture is heavy, it's worth having an electrician do it.
Q: How high should a chandelier hang over a dining table?
A: Usually 30 to 36 inches above the tabletop, a little higher with taller ceilings. We'll set it where it looks and works best.
Q: Do you supply the fixture?
A: We can, or we'll install the one you've chosen.

## Choosing the Fixtures

Slim LED light bars give even light with no hot spots and install neatly at the front of the cabinet. [LED strip lighting](@led-strip-lighting-installation) in an aluminum channel with a diffuser works well for a continuous line. Old-style puck lights create pools of light and are best for accents. We'll help you choose the color temperature to match your other lights and the brightness for your counters.

## Hardwired and Hidden

We run power from a nearby circuit into the cabinets and to a wall switch, often fishing the wire behind the backsplash or through the cabinets. Low-voltage systems need a driver, which we hide in a cabinet where it can still be reached. Everything goes on a switch or [dimmer](@dimmer-switch-installation), sometimes with the pendants or on a separate control. In a remodel, we rough in the wiring before the backsplash goes up.

## Part of a Kitchen Lighting Plan

Under-cabinet lighting works best as one layer of a plan: recessed lights for general light, [pendants](@pendant-lighting-installation) over the island, and under-cabinet lights for tasks. See [kitchen electrical upgrades](@kitchen-electrical-upgrade) for circuits, outlets and appliances.

## What Affects the Cost

Linear feet of cabinets, fixture type, whether walls and backsplash are finished and where the switch goes all shape the price.

## Placement and Color

Mounting fixtures toward the front edge of the cabinet lights the counter rather than the backsplash, and reduces reflections on shiny counters. Warm or neutral color temperatures usually match the kitchen's other lighting best. If your counters are polished stone, we'll choose fixtures with diffused lenses so you don't see a row of reflected dots. For glass-front cabinets, a separate strip inside the cabinet shows off what's on display. We can put those on their own switch or dimmer.

## LED Strip Lighting

Hardwired LED strip lighting in aluminum channels with diffusers gives an even line of light with no visible dots. We use it in coves, cabinets, shelves, stairs, bars and toe-kicks, with drivers sized for the run and tucked out of sight.

## Cove and Accent Lighting

Soft light in a tray ceiling or along crown molding, or accent light on shelves, artwork and built-ins, makes a room feel finished. We hide the drivers and put everything on a dimmer.

Q: Hardwired or plug-in?
A: Hardwired is cleaner, runs on a wall switch and doesn't use up your counter outlets.

LED floodlights use far less power than old halogen fixtures and last much longer. We choose brightness and beam spread for the area, and color temperature that looks good and helps cameras. Security lights can run dusk-to-dawn with a photocell, turn on with motion, or both: dim all night and full brightness on motion. They can also be scheduled or controlled from your phone with smart switches. We set the controls to match how you use your property.

## Low-Voltage Systems

Most landscape lighting runs on 12 volts from a transformer plugged into or wired to a GFCI-protected outdoor outlet. Low voltage is safer around the yard and lets fixtures be moved as plants grow. We size the transformer for the total load with room to add fixtures, plan cable runs to keep brightness even, and bury cable neatly so it doesn't get cut by edgers or aerators. If you need a new outlet for the transformer, see [outdoor outlet installation](@outdoor-outlet-installation).

## Designing the Layout

Good landscape lighting lights what matters and leaves some darkness for contrast. We walk the property with you, usually at dusk, and plan path lights, uplights on trees and walls, and step and deck lights. Warm LED color temperature looks best on stone, brick and plants. We aim fixtures to avoid glare into windows and neighboring yards.

## Timers and Controls

Transformers with photocells and timers, or astronomical clocks that follow sunset through the seasons, keep lights running without you touching them. Smart transformers can be controlled from your phone. For motion-activated or brighter lighting, see [security and flood lighting](@security-flood-lighting). Around pools, low-voltage lighting follows specific rules; see [pool electrical wiring](@pool-electrical-wiring).

## What Affects the Cost

The number of fixtures, the fixture quality, the size of the property and how the cable is routed drive the price. Many homeowners start with the front and add more later.

## Fixture Quality Matters

Landscape fixtures live outdoors year-round, so materials matter. Solid brass and copper fixtures last far longer than painted aluminum or plastic and develop a natural patina. Sealed LED fixtures resist moisture and insects. Quality connectors keep water out of connections, which is the most common cause of failed landscape lighting. We use fixtures and connectors built to last, and we'll show you what to expect from each option.

## Maintaining Your System

Plants grow, mulch builds up and fixtures get bumped. An occasional check keeps lights aimed and working, and we can adjust your system as your landscaping changes.

Q: Can I add more lights later?

:::signs

## Finding the Cause

Flicker has many causes: an incompatible [dimmer](@dimmer-switch-installation), a loose connection at the fixture or switch, a failing LED driver or a worn socket. Flicker across several rooms can point to a loose connection at the panel or service, which needs attention soon; see [electrical repair and troubleshooting](@electrical-repair-troubleshooting). We test the circuit, the switch and the fixture to find the cause, then fix it.

## Ballast and Bulb Replacement

Fluorescent fixtures use a ballast that eventually fails, causing flicker, humming or dark tubes. We can replace the ballast, but in many cases converting the fixture to LED makes more sense; see [LED lighting retrofits](@led-lighting-retrofits). We also replace bulbs in fixtures that are hard or unsafe to reach, like two-story foyers, cathedral ceilings and stairwells, and can switch them to long-life LEDs so you won't need us again for years.

## Outdoor and Landscape Lights

Outdoor fixtures face weather, and moisture causes corrosion and failures. We repair and replace porch lights, floodlights, post lights and landscape lighting, and fix tripped or failed transformers and photocells.

## What Affects the Cost

Most lighting repairs are quick once the cause is found. High ceilings, several fixtures and replacement parts affect the price, which we'll give you before doing the work.

## When to Replace Instead of Repair

If a fixture has corroded sockets, brittle wiring from years of heat, or cracked housings, replacing it is often cheaper than repairing it. Older fixtures in closets and attics may lack the protection that modern fixtures have. We'll tell you when a repair makes sense and when a new fixture is the better value, and give you prices for both.

## LED Retrofits

If your fixtures are old fluorescents, halogens or recessed cans, an LED retrofit often costs less over time than repairing them. We bypass ballasts, install LED retrofit kits or replace fixtures outright, in homes and small businesses.

## Lighting Repair Around Yardley

## FAQ
Q: Why do my lights flicker?
A: Usually a loose bulb, an incompatible dimmer, a loose connection or a failing driver. Flicker in several rooms can mean a problem at the panel.
Q: Should I replace my fluorescent ballast or switch to LED?
A: If the fixture is in good shape, either works. LED conversion usually costs about the same and ends ballast failures for good.
Q: Can you change bulbs in my two-story foyer?
A: Yes. We bring the equipment to work safely at height and can switch you to long-life LEDs.
Q: Why does my light bulb keep burning out?
A: Common causes are a loose socket, too much heat in an enclosed fixture, voltage problems or the wrong bulb type. We check the fixture and circuit to find it.
