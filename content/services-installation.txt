:::signs

- A breaker trips when a particular appliance runs with anything else
- Lights dim when the appliance starts up
- You're adding a freezer, wine fridge or second refrigerator
- You need a reliable circuit for a sump pump or medical equipment
- You're setting up a home office, gym, workshop or window AC

## What Gets a Dedicated Circuit

Code requires dedicated circuits for many built-in appliances, including dishwashers, disposals, built-in microwaves, and the washer and furnace. Beyond the rules, anything that draws a lot of power for a long time benefits from its own circuit: space heaters, window air conditioners, freezers, gym equipment and computer setups. Sump pumps and freezers get their own circuit so an unrelated trip doesn't leave you with a flooded basement or spoiled food.

## 120-Volt and 240-Volt Circuits

Most household equipment runs on 120-volt, 15 or 20 amp circuits. Larger equipment like ranges, dryers, water heaters, welders, air compressors, mini-splits and EV chargers needs a 240-volt circuit sized to the equipment. Our [appliance wiring](@appliance-wiring) page covers those hookups, and [EV charger installation](@ev-charger-installation) has its own page.

## How We Install It

We confirm your panel has space and capacity, size the wire and breaker for the load, and run the new circuit by the cleanest route, often from the basement or attic with a small opening for the new outlet. We add the ground-fault or arc-fault protection the location requires, label the breaker and test the circuit under load. If the panel is full or near capacity, we'll talk through a subpanel or [panel upgrade](@electrical-panel-upgrade).

## What Affects the Cost

Distance from the panel, the circuit's size, how hard the route is, and the type of breaker required all shape the price. A new 20 amp outlet near the panel is a quick job; a 240-volt run to a far corner of the house takes longer.

## Appliance Hookups

Ranges, cooktops, wall ovens, dryers, water heaters, dishwashers, disposals and built-in microwaves each need a circuit sized for that appliance and the right connection at the end of it. We wire new appliances, handle gas-to-electric and induction conversions, and check that the cord, receptacle and breaker all match the manufacturer's requirements.

## Kitchen Remodels

A remodel is the best time to get kitchen wiring right. Code calls for at least two 20-amp countertop circuits, GFCI protection, and dedicated circuits for the dishwasher, disposal, microwave and refrigerator. We plan island power and range circuits alongside [recessed](@recessed-lighting-installation) and [under-cabinet lighting](@under-cabinet-lighting-installation), and work around your contractor's schedule.

## Dedicated Circuits Around Yardley

We add circuits for workshops and home offices in [Warminster](@warminster), and split overloaded circuits in the original homes of [Levittown](@levittown) and [Fairless Hills](@fairless-hills).

## FAQ
Q: Does my refrigerator need its own circuit?
A: It's strongly recommended, and often required in new kitchens. A shared circuit can trip and leave your food warm.
Q: Can a space heater run on a regular outlet?
A: It can, but a 1,500-watt heater uses most of a 15-amp circuit by itself. That's the classic cause of tripping in bedrooms and offices.
Q: Do I need a permit for a new circuit?
A: Yes, a new circuit generally needs a permit. We handle it.

## Replacing Worn Switches and Outlets

When we replace a device, we don't just swap it. We check the box, the wire and the connections. Many older outlets were wired with push-in "backstab" connections that loosen over time and cause flickering, intermittent power and heat. We move those connections to the screw terminals or use quality back-wire devices, and replace any damaged wire ends. New outlets in homes are tamper-resistant, which keeps kids from pushing objects into the slots.

If a switch is warm or flickers, that's a sign of a loose connection or a device that's failing, and it should be fixed soon. See [electrical repair and troubleshooting](@electrical-repair-troubleshooting) if the problem is on more than one device.

A GFCI outlet shuts off power in a fraction of a second when it senses electricity taking a path it shouldn't, such as through a person standing near water. It's one of the most effective shock-prevention devices ever made, and one of the most common items on Yardley home inspection reports.

:::signs
## Where You Need GFCI Protection
- Kitchen countertop outlets and outlets near any sink
- Bathrooms and laundry areas
- Garages and unfinished basements
- All outdoor outlets, including decks and porches
- Near pools, hot tubs and wet bars
- Anywhere a home inspector flagged "missing GFCI"
:::

## GFCI Outlets vs. GFCI Breakers

There are two ways to protect a circuit. A GFCI outlet protects itself and every outlet wired after it on the circuit. A GFCI breaker in the panel protects the whole circuit from its source, which is useful when outlets are hard to reach, when a circuit feeds outdoor outlets, or for equipment like hot tubs. We'll choose the right method for each location, and label protected outlets downstream so you know where to reset.

## Installing and Testing

We identify the first outlet on each circuit, install GFCI protection there or at the panel, and test every outlet it covers. GFCIs can also protect ungrounded three-prong outlets when a ground wire isn't available, labeled "No equipment ground" as the code requires; see [ground wire installation](@ground-wire-installation-repair) for a permanent ground. For outdoor locations, see [outdoor outlet installation](@outdoor-outlet-installation). In bathrooms, we often pair GFCI work with [bathroom fixture wiring](@bathroom-fixture-wiring).

## GFCIs That Trip or Won't Reset

A GFCI that trips often may have found a real problem, such as moisture in an outdoor box, a worn appliance or damaged wiring. A GFCI that won't reset may have failed, or may have no power coming in. We find the cause. GFCIs also wear out and should be tested monthly with the test button.

## What Affects the Cost

GFCI outlets are affordable; GFCI breakers cost more. Most homes need only a handful, placed at the start of each circuit.

## Outdoor Outlets

Every outdoor outlet needs GFCI protection, a weather-resistant receptacle and an in-use cover that stays closed while something is plugged in. We add outlets for decks, patios and porches, holiday lights, landscape lighting transformers, pool equipment and yard tools, and replace cracked or rusted ones. An outdoor GFCI that trips every time it rains usually has water getting past a worn cover or a damaged receptacle, and we fix the cause instead of just resetting it.

## GFCI Work Around Yardley

We add GFCI protection as part of [code corrections](@electrical-code-corrections) before sales in [Southampton](@southampton) and [Langhorne](@langhorne), and in basements and garages in [Feasterville](@feasterville).

## FAQ
Q: Why did several outlets stop working at once?
A: Often a GFCI in another room, the garage or outside has tripped. Press its reset button. If it won't reset, call us.
Q: How often should I test my GFCIs?
A: Once a month. Press test, confirm power goes off, then press reset.
Q: Do GFCI outlets wear out?
A: Yes. Most last many years, but a GFCI that no longer trips when tested should be replaced.

## Fan-Rated Boxes Matter

A ceiling fan is heavy and it moves, and a standard light fixture box isn't built to hold it. Every fan we install is mounted to a fan-rated box or brace secured to the ceiling framing. When we replace a light with a fan, we check the existing box and replace it if it isn't rated, usually without opening the ceiling. That's the difference between a fan that runs quietly for years and one that wobbles or works loose.

## Adding a Fan Where There's No Fixture

Many older homes, especially 1950s homes in lower Bucks County, have bedrooms with no ceiling fixture. We run a new switch leg from the wall switch to the ceiling through the attic, install a fan-rated box and wire the fan. If you want the fan and light on separate wall switches, we run the extra conductor while we're there. Remote and wall-control kits are an option when adding wire isn't practical. For light-only fixtures, see [ceiling light installation](@ceiling-light-installation).

## Assembly, Balance and Setup

We assemble the fan, set the blade pitch, balance it so it runs without wobble and set the direction for the season. On high or sloped ceilings, we install the right downrod length and slope adapter. If you're ventilating a hot attic rather than cooling a room, see [attic fan installation](@attic-fan-installation).

## Porch and Outdoor Fans

## Attic Fans

## Bathroom Exhaust Fans and Fixtures

The distance from the panel to the charger, whether the run is inside or outside, the charger's amperage and your panel's capacity all affect the price. Detached garages may need a trench or a [garage subpanel](@shed-garage-wiring).

## Choosing Where to Mount the Charger

We'll look at where you park, where the charge port is on your car and whether you might add a second EV later. Mounting the charger where the cable reaches comfortably without crossing a walkway keeps it safe and convenient. Outdoor chargers need a location that's protected from snow piles and plows, and the charger itself must be rated for outdoor use.

## FAQ
Q: Do I need a permit for an EV charger?
A: Yes. A new 240-volt circuit for a charger needs a permit and an inspection, which we handle for you.
Q: Should I hardwire the charger or use an outlet?
A: Hardwiring is more reliable and allows higher charging speeds. A NEMA 14-50 outlet is more flexible if you might change chargers.
Q: Can my 100 amp panel handle an EV charger?
A: Sometimes, with a lower-amperage charger or a load-management device. The load calculation tells us for sure.
