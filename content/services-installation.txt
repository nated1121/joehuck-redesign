## What Gets a Dedicated Circuit

Code requires dedicated circuits for many built-in appliances, including dishwashers, disposals, built-in microwaves, and the washer and furnace. Beyond the rules, anything that draws a lot of power for a long time benefits from its own circuit: space heaters, window air conditioners, freezers, gym equipment and computer setups. Sump pumps and freezers get their own circuit so an unrelated trip doesn't leave you with a flooded basement or spoiled food.

## 120-Volt and 240-Volt Circuits

Most household equipment runs on 120-volt, 15 or 20 amp circuits. Larger equipment like ranges, dryers, water heaters, welders, air compressors, mini-splits and EV chargers needs a 240-volt circuit sized to the equipment. Our [appliance wiring](@appliance-wiring) page covers those hookups, and [EV charger installation](@ev-charger-installation) has its own page.

## How We Install It

We confirm your panel has space and capacity, size the wire and breaker for the load, and run the new circuit by the cleanest route, often from the basement or attic with a small opening for the new outlet. We add the ground-fault or arc-fault protection the location requires, label the breaker and test the circuit under load. If the panel is full or near capacity, we'll talk through a subpanel or [panel upgrade](@electrical-panel-upgrade).

## What Affects the Cost

Distance from the panel, the circuit's size, how hard the route is, and the type of breaker required all shape the price. A new 20 amp outlet near the panel is a quick job; a 240-volt run to a far corner of the house takes longer.

## Labeling and Documentation

Every new circuit gets a clear, typed label at the panel, so you know exactly what it feeds. That makes future troubleshooting faster and saves guessing during an outage.

## Dedicated Circuits Around Yardley

## FAQ
Q: Does my refrigerator need its own circuit?
A: It's strongly recommended, and often required in new kitchens. A shared circuit can trip and leave your food warm.
Q: Can a space heater run on a regular outlet?
A: It can, but a 1,500-watt heater uses most of a 15-amp circuit by itself. That's the classic cause of tripping in bedrooms and offices.
Q: Do I need a permit for a new circuit?
A: Yes, a new circuit generally needs a permit. We handle it.

A GFCI outlet shuts off power in a fraction of a second when it senses electricity taking a path it shouldn't, such as through a person standing near water. It's one of the most effective shock-prevention devices ever made, and one of the most common items on Yardley home inspection reports.

:::signs

- Near pools, hot tubs and wet bars
- Anywhere a home inspector flagged "missing GFCI"

## GFCI Outlets vs. GFCI Breakers

There are two ways to protect a circuit. A GFCI outlet protects itself and every outlet wired after it on the circuit. A GFCI breaker in the panel protects the whole circuit from its source, which is useful when outlets are hard to reach, when a circuit feeds outdoor outlets, or for equipment like hot tubs. We'll choose the right method for each location, and label protected outlets downstream so you know where to reset.

## Installing and Testing

We identify the first outlet on each circuit, install GFCI protection there or at the panel, and test every outlet it covers. GFCIs can also protect ungrounded three-prong outlets when a ground wire isn't available, labeled "No equipment ground" as the code requires; see [ground wire installation](@ground-wire-installation-repair) for a permanent ground. For outdoor locations, see [outdoor outlet installation](@outdoor-outlet-installation). In bathrooms, we often pair GFCI work with [bathroom fixture wiring](@bathroom-fixture-wiring).

## GFCIs That Trip or Won't Reset

A GFCI that trips often may have found a real problem, such as moisture in an outdoor box, a worn appliance or damaged wiring. A GFCI that won't reset may have failed, or may have no power coming in. We find the cause. GFCIs also wear out and should be tested monthly with the test button.

## What Affects the Cost

GFCI outlets are affordable; GFCI breakers cost more. Most homes need only a handful, placed at the start of each circuit.

## GFCI Breakers for Hard-to-Reach Circuits

When the first outlet on a circuit is behind a fridge, a washer or a basement freezer, resetting a GFCI outlet there is a nuisance. A GFCI breaker in the panel is often the better choice. We also avoid putting refrigerators and sump pumps on GFCI protection where the code allows, so a nuisance trip doesn't leave you with spoiled food or a wet basement.

## GFCI Work Around Yardley

We add GFCI protection as part of [code corrections](@electrical-code-corrections) before sales in [Southampton](@southampton) and [Langhorne](@langhorne), and in basements and garages in [Feasterville](@feasterville).

## FAQ
Q: Why did several outlets stop working at once?
A: Often a GFCI in another room, the garage or outside has tripped. Press its reset button. If it won't reset, call us.
Q: How often should I test my GFCIs?
A: Once a month. Press test, confirm power goes off, then press reset.
Q: Do GFCI outlets wear out?
A: Yes. Most last many years, but a GFCI that no longer trips when tested should be replaced.

## Dimmers for Fans and Special Fixtures

Ceiling fans need a fan-speed control, never a light dimmer, because a light dimmer can damage the motor. Low-voltage fixtures, some under-cabinet lights and LED strips need dimmers or drivers designed for their power supply. We'll match the control to each fixture, so everything works smoothly and lasts. If you have a mix of fixtures on one switch, we may recommend separating them so each can be controlled properly.

## Why LEDs Need the Right Dimmer

Older dimmers were designed for incandescent bulbs, which draw a lot of power. LEDs draw very little, and many older dimmers can't control such a small load smoothly. The result is flicker, buzzing and a limited dimming range. We install dimmers rated for LED loads and adjust their low-end trim settings so the lights dim smoothly without dropping out. We also check that the bulbs or fixtures themselves are dimmable, because not every LED is.

## Types of Dimmers We Install

We install standard slide, rotary and paddle dimmers, dimmers that work from more than one location, and smart dimmers from brands like Lutron that you can control from your phone or voice assistant. Smart dimmers usually need a neutral wire in the switch box, which some older homes don't have; we'll check and find a solution if yours doesn't. ## Pairing Dimmers With New Lighting

Dimmers matter most with [recessed lighting](@recessed-lighting-installation), chandeliers and dining room fixtures. When we install new lighting, we match the dimmer to the fixtures from the start. Replacing an ordinary switch is covered under [switch and outlet installation](@switch-outlet-installation).

## How Two-Way Switching Works

A 3-way circuit uses two special switches connected by two "traveler" wires, so either switch can change the state of the light. Adding a third or fourth location uses a 4-way switch between them. Installing a new 3-way location means running the right cable between the two switch boxes and the fixture, which we usually do from the attic or basement with minimal wall openings.

## Fixing 3-Way Circuits That Don't Work Right

The most common problem we see is a 3-way circuit that someone rewired incorrectly, so the light works only when the other switch is in one position. It's usually a matter of identifying the common and traveler terminals and correcting the connections. Worn 3-way switches can also cause intermittent problems and need replacing.

## Smart and Wireless Options

When running new cable isn't practical, smart switches with a wireless companion switch can control a light from a second location without new traveler wires. We install both the wired and the smart approach and will tell you which fits your home. For more, see [smart lighting installation](@smart-lighting-installation). You can also dim from both locations with the right [dimmer switches](@dimmer-switch-installation). If a switch just needs to move to a better spot, see [switch and outlet relocation](@switch-outlet-relocation).

## What Affects the Cost

The distance between switch locations, access through the attic or basement, and whether you choose wired or smart switches affect the price.

## Adding Switches During a Remodel

If walls are open for a remodel, it's the easiest time to add three-way switching at stairs, hallways and rooms with two doors. Adding the cable at that stage costs little, and it's one of the details that makes a house feel well planned.

## Equipotential Bonding

Bonding connects the pool's reinforcing steel, metal parts, deck and nearby metal so they're all at the same electrical potential. That way, a stray voltage can't create a difference between the water and the deck that a swimmer would feel. It's required for in-ground and many above-ground pools and must be installed and inspected before concrete is poured, so we coordinate closely with your pool builder.

## Equipment and Lighting

We run circuits to the equipment pad sized for the pump, filter, heater or heat pump, install a disconnect and GFCI protection where required, and make the connections. Pool lighting uses low-voltage LED fixtures and transformers or listed line-voltage fixtures installed to strict rules. Receptacles near the pool must be a set distance from the water and GFCI-protected; see [outdoor outlet installation](@outdoor-outlet-installation). ## Working With Your Pool Builder

Equipment is wired once the pad is ready, and final connections and testing happen before the pool is filled and started. We work from your builder's schedule and plans, and we're on site at each stage so nothing is missed. Above-ground pools need less work, but they still need bonding, a GFCI-protected pump circuit and a properly located outlet.
